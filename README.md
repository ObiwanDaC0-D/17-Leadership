# Leadership Learning Lab

A standalone, responsive GitHub Pages application for studying leadership as a relational, organizational, ethical and public-service process.

## V1 modules

- **59 leadership frameworks** across **10 domains**: leadership approaches, relationships and teams, organizations and systems, identity and influence, power and ethics, development and learning, change and decisions, equity and culture, public and civic leadership, and self-regulation and wellbeing.
- **Explore:** learning dashboard, domain discovery and study progress.
- **Theory Library:** searchable frameworks with central ideas, reading leads, practical applications and evidence cautions.
- **Concept Map:** domain-organized conceptual exploration.
- **Leadership Simulator:** compare five contexts and five leadership responses with qualitative tradeoffs; save experiments.
- **Decision Lab:** structure objectives, alternatives, evidence, uncertainty, stakeholders, tradeoffs, decisions and review triggers.
- **Team Dynamics:** design roles, psychological safety, conflict processes, inclusion and learning routines.
- **Communication Lab:** plan accountable, evidence-aware communication.
- **Social Scenarios:** six fictional leadership dilemmas with linked frameworks.
- **Compare:** side-by-side theoretical comparison.
- **Recall Lab:** sixteen retrieval prompts and heuristic review intervals.
- **Study Path:** six phases from foundational theories through public leadership and wellbeing.
- **Journal:** six guided reflective prompts.

## Deployment

The app is a single `index.html` with no build dependencies. To publish: **Settings → Pages → Deploy from a branch → main → /(root)**.

Expected Pages URL: https://obiwandac0-d.github.io/17-Leadership/

Learning records use browser localStorage and are not synchronized across devices. Export JSON from individual modules for backup. Avoid entering confidential workplace information.

## Evidence and limitations

The theory library provides foundational reading leads, not a verified systematic literature review. Leadership models differ in evidentiary support and applicability. The scenarios and simulator provide instructional, qualitative prompts; they do not measure leadership ability or predict outcomes.
